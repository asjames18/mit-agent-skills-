# Platform Playbooks

Use the section for the requested platform. When adapting one idea for multiple platforms, also follow the cross-platform transformation rules.

Platform features and limits change. Verify current publishing constraints against the platform's official help or creator documentation during the task. Do not rely on remembered character, duration, media-count, aspect-ratio, or scheduling limits.

## X

**Audience mindset:** Fast scanning, current conversation, distinct observations, and immediate utility.

**Choose a native format:** Single post, thread, reply, or quote-post commentary. Use a thread only when the idea genuinely needs ordered development.

**Drafting approach:**

- Make the first sentence carry the idea rather than introduce the topic.
- Prefer one point, concrete language, short paragraphs, and a closing line that lands.
- A useful pattern is observation → what is being missed → practical implication → strong close.
- Replies must add evidence, experience, a useful question, or a distinct perspective; never write generic praise.
- Quote-post commentary must stand on its own and explain why the referenced post matters.
- Treat media as part of the idea, not decoration. Avoid repeating the image text in the post.

**Avoid:** Bloated mini-articles, mechanical threads, constant hot takes, recycled replies, hashtag stuffing, context-free links, and engagement bait.

**Current constraint source:** [X Help — How to Post](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/how-to-post) and [X Help — Create a Thread](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/create-a-thread).

## LinkedIn

**Audience mindset:** Professional discovery, credibility, career relevance, useful implementation lessons, and evidence of judgment.

**Choose a native format:** Text post, image post, document carousel, native video, article, newsletter, or profile/Featured recommendation. Match depth to the format instead of forcing every idea into a text post.

**Drafting approach:**

- Use the opening lines to establish tension, evidence, or a useful outcome before the feed truncates the post.
- Ground the post in a real build, decision, problem, tradeoff, lesson, or supported industry change.
- A useful structure is context → action or analysis → evidence → lesson → why it matters to the reader.
- Translate technical work into organizational value without hiding the actual technical judgment.
- Use employer-safe abstraction. Remove internal architecture, private volumes, endpoints, incidents, and unsupported metrics.
- For document carousels, give each slide one job and build a coherent progression rather than splitting paragraphs across slides.
- Use a restrained professional CTA when a next step is useful; do not force a question onto every post.

**Avoid:** Resume recitation, corporate press-release language, false authority, generic career motivation, unsupported business outcomes, and copying an X post with extra paragraphs.

**Current constraint source:** [LinkedIn Help — Post and share updates](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a528176).

## Facebook

**Audience mindset:** Existing relationships, community conversation, shared identity, local relevance, personal context, and accessible storytelling.

**Choose a native format:** Feed post, photo post, link post, reel, story, event, or group post. Consider the selected audience and whether the content is for a personal profile, Page, or group.

**Drafting approach:**

- Write with more relational context and warmth than X or LinkedIn when the subject supports it.
- Lead with a human moment, shared concern, useful update, community benefit, or clear story.
- Explain why the subject matters without assuming technical background.
- Invite a specific, genuine conversation when community input would add value.
- For reels, design a mobile-first visual story with readable captions and key material kept clear of interface overlays.
- Check the audience selector and cross-post audience before any action; Facebook and Instagram audiences may differ.

**Avoid:** Treating Facebook as LinkedIn with a friendlier closing, publicizing private family or community information, vague inspirational filler, and assuming a cross-post reaches the same people.

**Current constraint sources:** [Facebook Help — Share something on Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/help/170116376402147), [Facebook Help — Reels audiences](https://www.facebook.com/help/204305118221546), and [Meta for Business — Reels creative](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/facebook-instagram-reels-ads).

## Instagram

**Audience mindset:** Visual discovery, identity, aspiration, education, demonstration, and saveable or shareable ideas.

**Choose the visual format before writing:** Single image, carousel, reel, or story. The asset carries the post; the caption supports it.

**Drafting approach:**

- Define the visual promise first: what should someone understand before reading the full caption?
- For carousels, use a clear cover, one idea per slide, progressive value, and a final takeaway or action.
- For reels, build vertically, open on the visual or verbal hook, use movement or demonstration, add on-screen text and captions, and keep essential elements inside safe zones.
- Write captions that add context, evidence, story, or instructions rather than transcribing the visual.
- Provide cover text, slide copy or shot list, caption, accessibility notes, and a restrained discovery strategy.
- Use hashtags only when they improve relevant discovery; do not substitute hashtags for positioning.

**Avoid:** Posting a text essay with an unrelated image, shrinking a LinkedIn post into a graphic, relying on audio without captions, overcrowded carousel slides, and unlicensed media.

**Current constraint sources:** [Meta for Business — Instagram and Facebook Reels](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/facebook-instagram-reels-ads) and [Meta Help — Instagram copyright guidance](https://www.facebook.com/help/354736791367645/).

## TikTok

**Audience mindset:** Discovery, entertainment, learning through demonstration, personality, participation, and fast visual payoff.

**Choose a native format:** Short vertical video, reply-to-comment video, demonstration, story, reaction, comparison, behind-the-scenes clip, or series episode.

**Drafting approach:**

- Start with the action, result, tension, or question; do not spend the opening introducing the creator.
- Use a hook → body → close structure, with the hook and visual payoff arriving immediately.
- Show the process or result when possible instead of explaining everything to camera.
- Write conversationally for spoken delivery. Use short sentences, natural pauses, on-screen text, and accurate captions.
- Deliver a complete production packet: concept, hook, shot list, spoken script or voiceover, on-screen text, caption, cover text, audio direction, CTA, and accessibility notes.
- Use trends only when they naturally support the message and the audio or media is licensed for the intended use.
- Favor native, human, mobile-first production over polished material that feels imported from another platform.

**Avoid:** Reposted horizontal video, slow scene-setting, watermarked cross-posts, trend-chasing without relevance, dense spoken paragraphs, and a caption that carries information missing from the video.

**Current constraint sources:** [TikTok for Business — Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/creative-codes) and [TikTok Creative Center — Creative tips](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/quicktok/online/5_creative_tips/pc/en).

## Cross-platform transformation

Keep the source fact, evidence, claim state, disclosure, and core point consistent. Deliberately change:

- Primary reader and their reason for using the platform
- Format and content depth
- Opening hook and pacing
- Amount and placement of context
- Visual concept, framing, and safe zones
- Spoken script, on-screen text, caption, or slide copy
- Proof placement and source presentation
- CTA and expected audience behavior
- Accessibility treatment
- Keywords, hashtags, mentions, and links

When a user asks for a multi-platform campaign, do not return five lightly edited captions. Return a platform plan and a native artifact for each channel.

## Platform-ready output

For each requested platform, include:

- Platform and recommended native format
- Primary reader and platform-specific objective
- Why this format fits the platform
- Hook or opening frame
- Exact copy, slide sequence, or spoken script
- Media concept, shot list, or asset requirements
- On-screen text, captions, alt text, and safe-zone notes when relevant
- Platform-appropriate CTA
- Current constraints checked and official source
- Risks, disclosures, and unresolved confirmations
